# Handoff

A student-to-student exchange for Wharton classmates. They list and find event tickets, trip spots and goods, post requests or private alerts, ask to buy and reserve, and arrange payment and the permitted transfer directly with each other. Handoff is a PennTools tool at `/tools/handoff`, built on the `team/handoff` branch as a pilot that hasn't opened to classmates yet. The [developer guide](#developer-guide) covers running and building it.

## Problem statement

Handoff is for Wharton students who need to buy, sell, or find a transferable club-event ticket when plans change or an event sells out. Today, they post offers and "in search of" (ISO) requests in WhatsApp or GroupMe, search chat history, and contact classmates individually to check availability and arrange a permitted transfer. Early requests can disappear before tickets become available, sold offers can remain visible, and students must separately check the event's eligibility and handoff requirements. The initial pilot will focus on one or two Wharton club events with confirmed transfer rules, bringing available tickets and outstanding requests into a shared, current view.

## Customer conversations

Three informal conversations with Wharton classmates who had recently tried to buy or sell a ticket are documented in the founder's November 1, 2026 PWIF research attachment, in the section "Three informal interviews." Participants are anonymized. The founder confirmed on November 2 that the following three experiences came from three different classmates.

- Classmate 1, seller: an offer received little interest when many classmates were selling, and the ticket eventually sold at a substantial loss.
- Classmate 2, buyer: requests for a sold-out party went unanswered, and a ticket was not found in time.
- Classmate 3, channel choice: posting in a large chat felt too public or spammy; asking classmates directly worked but required considerable effort.

Other reported context: scheduling clashes, recruiting, and late changes of mind created resale needs, while some events imposed transfer cutoffs. These are discovery conversations about existing behavior, not usability tests of Handoff or evidence of product adoption. The private PWIF application and raw chat exports are kept outside this repository.

## Current implementation

The page at `/tools/handoff` is a live, shared market. Listings, requests and exchanges are stored in Handoff's own database schema, and everyone sees them in real time under their PennTools account.

- **One account per person.** The PennTools session is the identity: there's no separate Handoff account and no buyer or seller mode. Buyer and seller are roles within each exchange.
- **Guests can browse.** Signed-out visitors get a read-only view that the server builds from published fields only, with no names, contact details, notes or budgets. Posting, asking to buy or making an offer, alerts and My activity ask for PennTools sign-in at that moment, then return to the same listing, request or form.
- **Tickets and trips: invite to pay** (since November 10, 2026). List tickets with one form and one action, "Post ticket" (a new event is added together with its listing; see Posting and browsing). A payment method is optional when posting: the Sell form has one compact row for it (the saved methods' names with Change, or "Add payment method", which opens the payment editor: an empty one opens on Venmo with its username field ready, Zelle is the other choice, and PayPal is offered only to a seller who saved it earlier, whose exchanges keep it; closing the editor saves nothing), and if none is saved, Handoff asks for one when the seller first invites a buyer, and keeps it in Profile, with Change.
  - **Request to buy.** The quantity is 1, with a choice only when the buyer may take more than one (what's available, within the event's per-person limit), and the total shows before anything is sent and again on the invitation, before "Reserve and pay". The buyer can add a short note (and an offer, where the seller takes offers). This holds nothing and shows no payment details.
  - **Invite to pay.** The seller confirms the quantity and price (for an offer, between the offer and the asking price). An invitation holds nothing either and isn't exclusive: the seller can invite other buyers, and the tickets go to the first invited buyer who reserves them. Requests and invitations stay open for 6 hours, or 1 hour close to the event, and end sooner at the transfer cutoff or a train's departure.
  - **Reserve and pay.** One step on the server checks the invitation, its terms, the buyer, the quantity, what's still available and the deadlines. Only then are the tickets held: for up to 15 minutes, less when a train leaves or the transfer window closes sooner, with the real deadline and where to pay. Two buyers reserving the last ticket at once get one hold.
  - **Then, as before.** The buyer pays the seller directly (Venmo or Zelle, or PayPal where the seller saved it earlier); the seller confirms the payment and transfers, and the buyer confirms receipt. The buyer can release an unpaid hold; the seller can't while it lasts. Either side can report a problem, and a buyer who paid after their hold ended can say so: the seller is told, and tickets another buyer has reserved since are never taken back. If the seller says a payment hasn't arrived, the buyer can cancel, and after a waiting time the seller can release the ticket.
  - **Free tickets** (since November 17, 2026). A ticket listed at $0 has nothing to pay, so no payment method is asked for it: not on Sell, not when the seller invites a buyer, and not on "List and invite". Its words invite nobody "to pay" ("You'll invite <first name> to take 1 ticket for free.", "Invite", "Reserve"). Reserving it skips payment entry and both payment steps: no payment details are copied or shown, nothing is marked paid or confirmed, and the reservation goes straight to the seller's step, "Send the ticket to" the buyer. The invitation (nothing held), the reservation (availability, the per-person limit, the member confirmation, emails shared from the reservation) and the receipt ("I've sent it", "Received") are as for any ticket. A free reservation is firm at once, so other invitations for the same ticket close then; until the seller sends it, the buyer can still release it, and it goes back on sale. A $0 reservation made before this keeps the steps it was made with.
  - Reservations and offers made before November 10 keep their terms and their steps.
- **Posting and browsing.** Adding an event shows only what it needs: an ordinary event's name and date (no time: it closes at 11:59 PM Philadelphia time that day), a train's one name and its departure (always an e-ticket), a trek's organizer, start and ticket types. The optional details (an event's or a train's face value, category, venue, link, notes and the other settings) are under one "Optional details" button. A face value, when given, caps every asking price, request budget and alert for that event; without one there's no cap, and sellers aren't asked what they paid. An ordinary event's category is optional and, when left out, is stored as not given, never guessed. Its organizer is optional too, except for a trek, a members-only event or one that checks names at the door. Selling or posting a request from an event reuses its details instead of asking for them again. While a name is typed in "Add an event", up to three upcoming events already on Handoff are suggested (the same name first; a date, once entered, refines the order), each with "Use this one", which returns to the form it was opened from (Sell a ticket or Request a ticket, for a trek) with that event chosen and what was typed there kept; separate seatings and departures stay separate choices, and adding another is never blocked. Event menus name each event's date, with its start or departure time when one is known (never a date-only event's 11:59 PM close), and date fields start empty. "Sell a ticket" is one form with one final action, "Post ticket" (since November 15, 2026). "What ticket are you selling?" is a search field: events already on Handoff show as the seller types, each its own row with its date, its time when one is known, its venue and organizer, and tapping one chooses it (from an event's page it's chosen already). Before anything is typed it lists the seller's own unlisted tickets first, then the soonest events; once they type, the best match comes first, whatever the word order and with half-typed words ("night oys" and "oys night" both find Oysters Night), and two seatings or departures stay two rows (since November 16, 2026). Nothing is chosen or merged for the seller: a typed name becomes a new event only when they tap "isn't one of these", or when nothing on Handoff resembles it, and then its date (a train: its departure) is asked right there. The form itself is the event, the asking price (it starts from the face value where the event gives one and can be changed) and one compact row for how buyers pay. Both fields are there before anything is typed (since November 16, 2026): a price the seller enters first stays as typed when they choose or change the event, $0 is a price, and a price above the chosen event's face value is said beside the price (and announced), never changed for them. A new ordinary event's date sits beside the price where they fit, and under each other on a narrow phone; a train keeps its departure date and time on its own row. Its required fields (the event's name, a new event's date, the asking price) carry an asterisk, with "(required)" in the field's name for screen readers. A new ordinary event's "Club or organizer (optional)" is on the form itself, right under its date (since November 18, 2026; it was under "Optional details" from November 16): it suggests the clubs already on Handoff as the seller types and takes any text. It becomes required in place, with the focus in it, when the seller chooses a restriction that needs a club (a members-only ticket, or an organizer name change) while none is named; an event already on Handoff shows its club in the chosen event's summary and never asks for it. Where an event on Handoff names no club, the seller may name one on their own listing, on the form under the chosen event (since November 18, 2026; under "Optional details" from November 17): it's kept on that listing only, never written to the event, and buyers see it as plain text beside the listing and find the event under it in By club. A club is informational: a ticket for a GCC event is open to everyone unless a real restriction applies, and buyers see no warning for it. A club is saved trimmed, and a name that's already a club on Handoff is saved in that club's own spelling whatever capitals, spacing or punctuation were typed ("gcc" is saved as GCC), so one club stays one club (since the final batch, November 17, 2026). By club and the search read the saved affiliation: an event's own club, or, where it names none, the club named on one of its listings that's still up. The quantity (1), offers, the note and a new event's own details are under "Optional details", and so is one unticked entry, "This ticket has restrictions" (since November 17, 2026, in place of "Members-only ticket" and "Flag a transfer restriction"). Ticked, the seller picks what applies: members only, an organizer name change ("The organizer must change the name on the ticket"), or something else in a few words of their own (3 to 80 characters). Buyers read each in those plain words ("GCC members only") on the listing, in "Request to buy" before they send it and on the invitation before they reserve, and still confirm membership, which the server still requires; when nothing is restricted they read nothing about restrictions, and no page says "Rules not confirmed" any more. - **My activity.** What needs your attention, plus active and past listings, purchases, requests and alerts. Sellers edit and close their listings and items here, and items from their own page too; an ordinary ticket's listing can also be managed on its event's page ("Your listing"), which is where the seller's own shared link opens. Manage listing for an ordinary ticket leads with the event, and one status line ("1 ticket available · $25", with held and transferred counts only when there are some); a buyer who is waiting for the seller comes first, and the history stays collapsed. It names no delivery method until sending is the next step: after the seller confirms the payment it says "Send the ticket to" the buyer, with "I've sent it". A listing that recorded a method keeps it on its exchanges, and an organizer name change, a members-only ticket and a train's or trek's rules show as before. An edit changes only what happens next: requests, invitations, reservations and offers already made keep their prices, and the price can't go below an open invitation or offer. An active ordinary ticket listing has no Pause (one paused earlier can be resumed or closed); train and trek listings and Goods items can still be paused, which takes them off sale for a while: exchanges already agreed carry on, and open requests, invitations and offers stay open, with their own deadlines, but nobody can be invited, reserve or have an offer accepted until it's resumed. Closing ("Close listing", "Close item") takes what's left off sale for good, in one step with an optional reason only the seller sees ("Sold elsewhere" or "No longer selling"); the step says how many pending requests and invitations it ends, reservations and payments already made go on, a held item can't be closed until its hold ends, and closing never counts as a sale. "List again" or "Post again" brings back the form, filled in. Requests close in one press ("No longer looking", which is also on the event's page, or Mark fulfilled), with "Post again"; a request for several tickets that gets some of them stays open for the rest.

Handoff doesn't take payments or deliver tickets: it records what both people confirm. The server checks every change (who may make it, what's still available, the last unit) and sends each person only what they may see.

**Status:**
- **Verified so far:** on a local server, through the real page, signed in with labelled TEST accounts and minted local sessions.

### How it's built

Handoff uses the User and Database APIs, not the AI API. Section 4 of [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) explains both, and **Tool database isolation** in its section 5 has the database rules.

**Page** (`apps/web/src/app/tools/handoff/`)

- **`page.tsx`** is a server component. It gets the user with `getCurrentUser()` and the database client with `toolSql("handoff")`, then serves one of three things:
  - signed in: that person's view;
  - signed out: the guest view;
  - no database, or a missing `handoff` role or schema: `HandoffUnavailable`, which says what's missing. It never falls back to sample data.
- **`actions.ts`** holds the five server actions the page calls: `act` (every change without a photo), `refresh`, `markRead`, `saveMyProfile` and `deleteMyData`. Each gets the user itself and returns errors as data.
- **`act/route.ts`** (POST only) takes a change that carries a photo ("Post item" with one, or a new photo for an item), never a server action: it reads the body with a size cap (`readBodyText`, one photo as base64 and the action) and answers what `act` would.
- **`Handoff.tsx`** mounts the UI, styled by `Handoff.module.css`:
  - `HandoffPrototype.jsx`: the app shell and the pages not yet in a module of their own, such as discovery, the event page, the posting forms, Wanted and request pages, Help, the guest pages and the sign-in prompts;
  - `_components/`: `exchanges.jsx` (reservations, offers, and the seller's listing panel with Edit and Close), `goods.jsx` (items, with their page and panel), `requests.jsx` (requests and alerts), `activity.jsx` (My activity) and `ui.jsx` (shared pieces);
  - `_lib/`: `live-link.ts`, the link to the server actions, typed against the server's own types, which refreshes when the tab gets focus, every minute while it's visible, and when the next deadline it shows arrives; `routes.js`, one table of pages with the sign-in return targets, tested in `routes.test.ts`; `session.js`, which keeps an open form when a session ends, for the same account only; `confirm.ts`, which shows nothing of an account's page after an account switch until the server confirms who is signed in; `sell.js` and `manage.js`, the rules of the Sell and Request forms and of Manage listing that need no page, tested in `sell.test.ts`, `k2-forms.test.ts`, `l2-sell.test.ts` and `manage.test.ts`; `photo.js`, which resizes a chosen Goods photo in the browser and makes a posted photo's address, tested in `photo.test.ts`; `look.js`, the picture a card leads with on Available and Wanted (a stated category's icon, a cue in the title or the neutral ticket; a Goods seller's photo or the item's category icon), `cards.js`, the one card layout's words (the owner's "Your listing" label, its status and the price a card leads with among them, and the clubs a card's club line names with By club's key for each), and `wanted.js`, a Wanted card's lines and a signed-out visitor's Wanted, tested in `look.test.ts`, `m4-cards.test.ts`, `n2-cards.test.ts`, `o1-club.test.ts` and `wanted.test.ts`; `call-gate.ts`, which holds the page's address writes until its calls to the server have answered, tested in `m5-call-gate.test.ts`; `profile.js`, Handoff settings' words (the public-name line, "Who can see my details?", "Tickets you've added"), tested in `m5-profile.test.ts`; `plain.js`, the plain words that replaced the routine "not verified" captions (an exchange's steps by who acted, the Payment row, an event's source, Help's one explanation), tested in `n3-plain.test.ts`, with the optional club's rules (a club as it's saved, what Request sends for a new event, a visitor's By club) tested in `n3-club.test.ts`; and `mode.js`, `format.js` and `dom.js`.
- **`world.ts`** picks the market. That's the shared `live` world, except in development builds, where the `handoff-world` cookie can name a `test-…` world; the browser checks use test worlds to stay out of the shared market.
- **`photo/[item]/route.ts`** serves a Goods item's one photo (GET only) to whoever sees the item at that moment: a signed-out visitor while it's available, a signed-in account until it ends. Anything else is a bare 404. It reads only the item's own rows, and a browser that already holds the photo gets a 304 without its bytes being read; the answer is `no-cache`, so a photo stops showing as soon as its item does.
- **`preview/`** and **`_preview/`** are the sample-data demo, served in development builds only. The live page doesn't download them.
- **No outbound email.** Handoff's own email (the Resend integration, its dispatch route, scheduler and settings) was removed in the review of PR #88: a tool may not send email itself. Notifications are in the app (the bell and My activity).

**Logic** (`tools/handoff/`)

- **`engine/`** holds the marketplace rules. `index.js` is one reducer, used by the page in the browser and by the server; `time.js` reads every date and time as New York time; `preview.js` holds the preview's sample data and the original rule self-tests, which only the preview and the tests import.
- **In `src/`:**
  - `live.ts` checks every action's input.
  - `store.ts` keeps the market as rows (one per event, holding, listing, exchange, request, share, item and goods request) and runs each change in one transaction that locks only what it touches: the rows it changes, the rows they depend on (an exchange's listing, a goods request's item) and the profiles of the people it makes hold something. So two buyers can't both get the last unit, and unrelated trades go through at the same time. It records request IDs, so a retried click is applied once; limits each account (for the pilot: 100 open records, counted as the page shows them, so a sold-out listing or an item past its last day doesn't count; and 30 changes a minute); and settles expired holds and deadlines whenever the market is read or written. Each app instance keeps one copy of the market per version for all its tabs, and reads only the rows that changed since.
  - `project.ts` builds each person's view, and the guest view, from allowlists, so private fields never leave the server.
  - `handlers.ts` holds the server actions' logic, and `index.ts` exports what the page folder uses.
  - `engine-port.ts` is the store's only way into the engine, `types.ts` declares every field a view can carry, and `db-errors.ts` turns database errors into plain messages (not set up, unavailable, busy).
  - `report/` is the [pilot metrics report](#pilot-metrics-report), which `scripts/pilot-report.mjs` runs. The web app doesn't import it.
- **`schema.sql`** holds seven tables (and drops the four email tables earlier versions made):
  - `worlds`: each market's settings and version (a world written before the rows held its whole market here, until its first write split it into rows, after a before-upgrade snapshot);
  - `records`: the market itself, one row per record, with its place in its list, its revision and its owner;
  - `people`: names, emails and payment methods (Venmo, Zelle or PayPal; a handle saved before them is kept as it was), saved by an account's first change and deleted with "Delete my Handoff data";
  - `notifications`;
  - `actions`: recent requests, so a retry gets the first answer, kept for 7 days;
  - `world_snapshots`: short-lived rollback points (each a whole market as one document; to roll back, in one transaction put one in `worlds.doc`, set `split_at` to NULL and `version` to at least the world's current version (`worlds.version` plus the sum of its records' `tally`), and delete the world's `records`: the next write splits it into rows again);
  - `item_photos`: a Goods item's one optional photo (a JPEG or WebP of at most 300 KB, one row per item), written in the same transaction as the item and deleted when the photo is removed or the item is sold, closed or expires. The market's JSON holds only a reference to it (`src/photo.ts` checks what gets in).
- **Tests** are in `src/__tests__/`. The database tests use `DATABASE_URL` from the environment or `apps/web/.env.local`, with throwaway schemas, and are skipped without one.

**Database rules** (`tools/handoff/schema.sql`)

- `schema.sql` is Handoff's whole schema, and it runs again on every deploy, so every statement must be safe to re-run: `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`, `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS`, and, for a column added later, `ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`. Editing a `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` statement later changes nothing where the table already exists, so make other changes to an existing table, such as a new value in a `CHECK`, with `ALTER TABLE` statements that are safe to re-run, for example `ALTER TABLE world_snapshots DROP CONSTRAINT IF EXISTS world_snapshots_reason_check, ADD CONSTRAINT world_snapshots_reason_check CHECK (reason IN (…))`.
- Follow AskPenn's `schema.sql`: table names without a schema prefix; user IDs as `TEXT NOT NULL`, with no foreign key to the platform's `users` table, which Handoff's database role can't read; and a `TEXT` column with a `CHECK (… IN (…))` constraint for a fixed set of values, such as a snapshot's reason, rather than `CREATE TYPE … AS ENUM`, which has no `IF NOT EXISTS` and fails on the next deploy.
- `page.tsx` and the server actions get the database client from `toolSql("handoff")` in `@penntools/platform/db`, the only `@penntools/platform` import the page folder may use; `tools/handoff/src/` imports nothing from `@penntools/platform`. `toolSql` returns null when `DATABASE_URL` is missing or invalid, so check for that, as AskPenn's `page.tsx` and `actions.ts` do.
- The tests connect as the database's admin user, so they don't catch statements Handoff's role isn't allowed to run, such as a foreign key to `public.users`. `db:deploy` does, and so do the steps in [Database setup](#database-setup).

### Database setup

Handoff's tables live in the `handoff` schema, owned by a `handoff` database role.

- **How it's normally created:** the platform's `db:deploy` creates both and applies `schema.sql`, for the tools listed in `TOOLS` in `packages/platform/scripts/setup-tools.mjs`. `scripts/setup.sh` runs `db:deploy`, and so does Railway before each deploy.
- **Handoff's line** is `{ name: "handoff", dir: "tools/handoff", connectionLimit: 30 },`. The Platform team adds it, and it must land with or after `schema.sql` on the same branch: a listed tool without a `schema.sql` makes `db:deploy` fail, which blocks the whole deploy.
- **Until the role exists,** `toolSql("handoff")` still returns a client, but its queries fail, and the page says Handoff isn't set up.

Once the line is on `team/handoff`, apply the schema locally, and again after every change to `schema.sql`:

```bash
DATABASE_URL="postgresql://penntools:penntools@localhost:5432/penntools" pnpm --filter @penntools/platform db:deploy
```

In PowerShell, set the variable first, with `$env:DATABASE_URL = "postgresql://penntools:penntools@localhost:5432/penntools"`, and then run `pnpm --filter @penntools/platform db:deploy`.

**Until then, create them by hand** on your local database, with the same statements `setup-tools.mjs` runs:
- Run them in Git Bash (or a macOS or Linux shell), from the repository root, while the `penntools-db` container is running.
- They're safe to re-run. On a re-run, "already exists, skipping" notices are expected.

```bash
pw=$(node -e "process.loadEnvFile('apps/web/.env.local'); const p = decodeURIComponent(new URL(process.env.DATABASE_URL).password); process.stdout.write(require('crypto').createHmac('sha256', p).update('penntools-tool-role:handoff').digest('base64url'))")
docker exec -i penntools-db psql -U penntools -d penntools -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 <<SQL
SELECT 'CREATE ROLE handoff LOGIN' WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM pg_roles WHERE rolname = 'handoff') \gexec
ALTER ROLE handoff WITH LOGIN NOSUPERUSER NOCREATEDB NOCREATEROLE CONNECTION LIMIT 30 PASSWORD '$pw';
ALTER ROLE handoff SET search_path = handoff;
CREATE SCHEMA IF NOT EXISTS handoff AUTHORIZATION handoff;
SQL
docker exec -i penntools-db psql -U handoff -d penntools -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 --single-transaction < tools/handoff/schema.sql
```

- **What each part does:**
  - The first line derives the role's password from the one in `DATABASE_URL`, as the platform's `toolSql("handoff")` does, so the app can log in.
  - The heredoc creates the role and schema, as the admin user.
  - The last line applies `schema.sql` as the `handoff` role, in one transaction. Re-run it after every change to `schema.sql`.
- **To check:** this prints `handoff: actions, item_photos, notifications, people, records, world_snapshots, worlds`:
  `docker exec penntools-db psql -U handoff -d penntools -tAc "SELECT current_schema() || ': ' || string_agg(tablename, ', ' ORDER BY tablename) FROM pg_tables WHERE schemaname = current_schema() GROUP BY current_schema()"`
- **To start over:** as the admin user, `DROP SCHEMA handoff CASCADE; DROP ROLE handoff;` deletes Handoff's local data.

**What the platform doesn't provide, and what Handoff does instead**

- **Names and contact details.** None of the APIs a tool may use returns another user's name or email, and there's no email, messaging or notification API. So Handoff keeps each person's name and email from `getCurrentUser()` in its `people` table, saved by their first change (opening Handoff saves nothing) and refreshed by each change after, along with the payment methods they save; "Delete my Handoff data" removes it. It shows these only to the people that person is dealing with, at the step that needs them: a buyer sees which kinds a seller takes before committing, and where to pay only once it's reserved for them, from a copy kept with that exchange. Its notifications are its own, in the app.
- **Scheduled jobs.** There are none, so nothing expires by itself. Handoff settles expired holds, offers and deadlines whenever the market is read or written, and notifies people in the app as part of the change that causes it.
- **Who can sign in.** Sign-in admits any verified Penn Google account (`upenn.edu` or a subdomain such as `wharton.upenn.edu`), not just Wharton students. A Wharton-only rule needs a check in Handoff's own code on the user's email, the only school signal `getCurrentUser()` gives. That check would also shut out the local "Local Developer" user, whose email is `dev@localhost`.
- **Signed-out visitors.** They can open every page, so Handoff serves them a separate guest view, built on the server from published fields only.
- **File storage.** There is none: PennTools has no shared image or file storage a tool may use. So a Goods item's one optional photo is kept in Handoff's own `item_photos` table (at most 300 KB, one per open item, deleted when the item ends) and served by a route in Handoff's page folder with the item's own visibility. That's space in the shared database, so it's worth a line to the Platform team with the PR; a storage API for tools, if the platform adds one, would replace the table.

### Pilot metrics report

A small report for the team from the records Handoff already keeps: participants, listings and requests, current stock, each step of invite to pay (requests to buy, invitations, holds, the buyer's and the seller's payment steps, completed exchanges), reservations and completed exchanges, cancellations, closures, expiries and disputes, timings, and supply and demand by event and category. An event added without a category is counted as "(unspecified)". It's not a dashboard and adds no tracking. Run it from the repository root, with the database running and the package built (`pnpm dev` keeps it built; otherwise run `pnpm --filter @penntools/tool-handoff build`):

```bash
node tools/handoff/scripts/pilot-report.mjs              # JSON, then a short Markdown summary
node tools/handoff/scripts/pilot-report.mjs --markdown   # Markdown only (--json for JSON only)
```

- **Read-only:** every read runs in one `REPEATABLE READ, READ ONLY` transaction, on a connection whose transactions default to read-only. It creates no profile, settles no deadline and marks nothing read.
- **Aggregate only:** counts, sums, durations, fixed words and event IDs. It never prints a name, email, payment method or handle, account ID, alert, private budget or offer, message, note or title.
- **Local only:** it reads `DATABASE_URL` from `apps/web/.env.local` and refuses any host other than localhost, a production `NODE_ENV`, a stale build and unknown options.
- **The real market only:** it reads the `live` world, never a test world, and leaves the TEST and Local Developer accounts out of every activity figure. Money is "user-confirmed exchange value", not verified payments. A request to buy or an invitation holds nothing, so neither counts as a hold or a sale, and a closed listing never counts as a sale. Visits, searches and sign-ins aren't recorded, so they're never reported.

Every report states its definitions, its window and what it can't cover.
